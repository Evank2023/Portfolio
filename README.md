# Blue Pockt Online Shopping Mall Paid Search Campaign Marketing

A fictitious US shopping mall named "Blue Pocket" aims to evaluate their marketing campaigns' performance using 5 months of data from 2021. Their promotions include paid search coupons and promo codes.

**Dataset Dictionary:**

- **Ad Group:** Category of the advertisement (e.g., coupon/promo code, desktop ad/mobile ad).
- **Month:** Campaign month. The campaign ran from July 2023 to November 2023.
- **Impressions:** Digital marketing metric quantifying the number of views or engagements an advertisement receives, also known as an **ad view**.
- **Clicks:** Number of clicks the ad received.
- **CTR:** Click-Through Rate—the number of clicks an ad receives divided by the number of times it's shown (**clicks ÷ impressions = CTR**).
- **Conversions:** Valuable actions users take on a website, such as making a purchase or filling out a form. Success is measured by the number of conversions generated at a **particular cost**.
- **Conv Rate:** Conversion Rate—the percentage of people who convert after clicking on ads. Conversions may include making a purchase, completing a contact form, or requesting a **free trial**, depending on campaign goals.
- **Cost:** Actual money spent by the advertiser (the "shop") for the related ad group.
- **CPC:** Cost Per Click—the cost of specific ads divided by the number of clicks. This metric evaluates campaign effectiveness in terms of ROI (Return on Investment). A lower or decreasing CPC is preferable to a higher or increasing one.
- **Revenue:** Total income generated by the advertisement.
- **Sale Amount:** Revenue of sales derived from a single ad group.
- **P&L:** Profit and Loss, calculated using the formula **Revenue - Cost**.

---

Over a 5-month period, the campaign generated $12.05M in sales but resulted in a total profit margin of -$73.41K. The return on ad spend was 0.88, and the return on investment was -0.12. Despite a near-profitable margin in October 2023, the overall campaign was not profitable.

**Mobile vs Desktop**

Search targets had greater exposure on mobile, with 1.92M impressions compared to 0.8M on desktop. Despite mobile impressions being double that of desktop, sales amounts were relatively close: $6.5M on mobile and $5.6M on desktop. To understand this discrepancy, we analyzed click and conversion rates. Mobile had 0.7M clicks with a 0.06 conversion rate, while desktop had 0.2M clicks with a 0.1 conversion rate. Although mobile had 0.5M more clicks than desktop, desktop's conversion rate was 0.04 higher, suggesting customers are more likely to purchase on desktop than mobile.

**Targets**

The one-to-one matching target had the highest exposure with 1.8M impressions, followed by the Exact keyword target with 0.5M and the Phrase target with 0.4M. This trend was mirrored in Sales Amount: one-to-one matching performed best with $8.8M, followed by Exact keyword at $2M and Phrase at $1.2M.

- The **one-to-one matching** target was used for five promotions: Shop Coupon, Shop Promo Code, Shop Coupon Code, Shop Discount Code, and Shop Promo.
- The **Exact keyword** target was used for eight promotions: Promo Code, Coupon Code, Discount Code, Offer, Sales, Competitor, Free Shipping, and Black Friday/Cyber Monday.
- The **Phrase** target was used for seven promotions: Promo Code, Coupon Code, Sale, Discount Code, Offer, Competitor, and Free Shipping.

The one-to-one matching and Exact keyword targets didn't overlap in promotions, while the Phrase target followed the same promotions as the Exact keyword target, except for Black Friday/Cyber Monday.

As the one-to-one matching target was personalized for each potential online client, it had noticeably higher exposure than other targets. The click-through rates were 0.39 for one-to-one matching, 0.26 for Exact, and 0.21 for Phrase. Interestingly, the conversion rate was identical at 0.08 for all three targets.

The one-to-one target had the highest average CPC at $0.9, followed by Exact keyword at $0.8 and Phrase keyword target at $0.7. However, the most expensive average cost per acquisition was the Phrase target at $7.9, followed by Exact keyword target at $7 and one-to-one matching target at $6.4.

**Promotions**

The highest Sales Amount per promotion was $3.5M for Shop Promo Code, followed by Shop Coupon at $2.5M, Shop Coupon Code at $1.9M, Promo Code at $1.7M, and Coupon Code at $1M. Lower-performing promotions included Shop Discount at $0.4M, Discount Code and Offer at $0.2M each, Shop Promo at $0.1M, Sale at $0.09M, Competitor at $0.03M, Free Shipping at $0.01M, and Black Friday/Cyber Monday at $0.004M.

The profit margin was negative for all promotions except Black Friday/Cyber Monday, which had a $0.2K profit margin. The most underperforming promotion was Shop Coupon with -$23.9K, followed by Shop Promo Code at -$12.6K and Shop Coupon Code at -$10.4K. Despite generating substantial sales, the overall campaign yielded negative returns.

**Return on Ad Spend**

Black Friday/Cyber Monday significantly outperformed other promotions with a Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) of 4.36. The remaining promotions had lower ROAS, ranging from 0.94 for Promo Code to 0.4 for Sale. Notably, only Black Friday/Cyber Monday returned more than four times the ad spend.

**Outlier: Black Friday/Cyber Monday**

As Black Friday and Cyber Monday are infrequent events, this promotion ran only in November 2023. Despite contributing just $4K in Sales Amount (0.08% of total), it achieved a remarkably high 4.36 ROAS.

**Conclusion**

Overall, the campaigns incurred more costs than revenue. The negative profit margin of -$73.41K suggests that maximizing profits wasn't the primary focus. This could indicate a different objective, such as inventory reduction. The campaigns generated significant sales of $12.05M, aligning with a possible goal of moving large quantities of inventory quickly, even at the cost of profitability. The variety of promotion types used (coupons, promo codes, discounts) is consistent with aggressive sales tactics often employed to clear inventory.

However, this remains a hypothesis. Without explicit information about the company's goals, we can't definitively state that inventory reduction was the primary objective. Other possibilities, such as market penetration or customer acquisition, could also explain the willingness to operate at a loss. To strengthen this argument, we'd need additional data on inventory levels or explicit statements about the campaign's objectives.

Search keywords had greater exposure on mobile, with twice as many impressions as desktop. However, the conversion rate was higher on desktop. To boost promotions, it might be beneficial to focus on the mobile platform, leveraging its high impression volume while working to improve its lower conversion rate.

Among the three targets, the one-to-one target is the most efficient, spending less while having the highest conversion rate. The Exact keyword target is the second most efficient, followed by the Phrase target.

This dataset lacks information about specific keywords or phrases used. Additionally, if detailed information about the one-to-one match were available, we could conduct a more precise analysis to understand which phrases and settings for one-to-one matching could improve campaign performance.
